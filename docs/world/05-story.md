# 5. Story options

## What the story has to do

- Explain in ten seconds why fixing writing fixes the world.
- Work for every later subject without a rewrite.
- Never threaten, punish or hurry. Nothing can be lost, so there is no villain and no clock.
- Stay out of the way. An easily distracted learner will not read a saga.

## Option A: The Dimming (recommended)

**The idea.** In Aurelune, light runs on meaning. Every gate, bridge and lamp is powered by an inscription, and it only works when the words are clear and correct. Long ago a soft drift passed through and smudged the inscriptions. Nothing was destroyed. The city simply went dim and has been waiting.

**Your arrival.** The First Gate at the castle is the oldest inscription of all. It flickers once, and you step out of it. No one has come through in an age. Queen Amaris takes this as a sign: whoever the Gate chose can read what the city has forgotten.

**The first task.** The Gate's inscription is muddled. You repair it. The Gate blazes gold, and across the water one distant lamp answers. That lamp is your next place.

**Why it is my pick:**

- **Learning is the literal mechanism.** You do not earn a reward for writing well. Writing well is what turns the lights on.
- **It stretches to every subject.** Words power the lamps. Numbers keep the islands level. Fair trades keep the harbor's tides steady. Memory keeps the paths from fading. Each realm gets its own kind of "clarity" with no new plot.
- **It has no villain and no decay.** Dim is not dying. What you light stays lit. This fits your no-punishment rule in the story itself.
- **It is short.** The whole premise fits in three speech bubbles.
- **It makes progress visible.** Your city goes from blue-violet to gold.

**Weakness:** it is gentle. There is no dramatic stake. I think that is right for an app you open several times a day, and the ending hook below adds a pull.

**A light mystery for the long run.** Why did the Gate choose you, and where does its twin stand? Each new portal you open reveals one line of an old message, written by whoever built the gates. It is one sentence at a time, weeks apart, and never required reading.

## Option B: The Invited Scholar

**The idea.** Aurelune is thriving. Queen Amaris invites promising scholars from other worlds to study in her city and build a district of their own. You are this season's guest. The castle gate is dim only because it is yours, and each scholar must light their own.

**Strengths:** the lowest stakes and the closest to Township. Building your own district is a clear, cozy goal.

**Weaknesses:** learning is back to being schoolwork you do for a reward. The world does not need you, so there is less reason to return. Later subjects feel like "more classes".

## Option C: The Twin Gate

**The idea.** Every gate has a twin somewhere else, and the two always act as one. The castle gate's twin stood in your world, which is how you fell through. Something has knocked the pairs out of step. You repair gates to bring pairs back together, searching for the way home, and slowly learn you may not want to leave.

**Strengths:** the strongest plot and the best use of your quantum theme.

**Weaknesses:** it needs the most reading and the most writing to pay off. "Trying to get home" quietly says this place is somewhere to escape. A mystery this size sets up an ending, and your world is meant never to end.

## My recommendation

**Option A, with the twin gates from C as scenery and the warmth of B in how you are treated.** You are a guest the city is glad to have, the lights come back because of you and the twin-portal mystery ticks along one line at a time for those who like it.

## The first two minutes, beat by beat

All of this is skippable, and each line of speech is one or two short sentences.

1. **Arrival (about 8 seconds).** Stars streak past and fold into an archway. You are standing on a marble terrace. The camera rises slowly: floating islands, waterfalls, a ringed planet filling the sky.
2. **The castle (about 5 seconds).** The camera settles on the castle and the tall dim archway in its courtyard, the First Gate.
3. **Amaris (two bubbles).** "You came through the First Gate. No one has done that in an age." Then: "Its inscription has slipped out of true. Will you look?"
4. **The task.** The scene dims, the calm card rises and you repair the inscription: five small items.
5. **The payoff (about 4 seconds).** Each fix lit one glyph. With the fifth, the Gate floods gold, its ghost copies slide into one and coins arrive with a one-line reason.
6. **The pointer.** Far off, one lamp answers. Amaris: "The Message Spire heard you." Her light drifts toward it.
7. **Your choice.** Step through the Gate, walk the terrace path or go back to Bloom's home screen. Nothing forces an order.

## Words the world uses

A small fixed vocabulary keeps reading short:

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Light** (verb) | To restore a place or finish a task |
| **Dim** | Not yet restored |
| **Inscription** | The words that power something |
| **The First Gate** | The castle portal, your travel hub |
| **Isle** | One floating island |
| **Realm** | A group of isles for one subject |
