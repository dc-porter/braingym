# 7. Growth and economy

## Real numbers from the code

**There are none.** The audit confirmed, and I checked again, that Bloom has no coins, rewards or prices anywhere in its code. Every number below is a proposed starting default, labeled as an estimate, to be tuned once the first module and the coin system exist, as the Log says.

## The rules, restated

All of these are yours. I changed none.

- Coins come only from finished learning tasks.
- A fresh task pays in full. A spaced review pays in full. An identical repeat pays about half once, then nothing, shown as "already earned".
- Nothing pays less for doing worse. Bonuses reward growth only and stay smaller than base pay.
- Coins fund world growth. Small expansions open with coins. Big portals and new destinations open at milestones and cannot be bought.
- Crystals buy cosmetic and fun items only. They never buy expansions or buildings.
- Missed days pause growth and never undo it. No streaks. No timers.
- A placement stop pays the same whatever the score.

## Two wallets

| | Coins | Crystals |
|---|---|---|
| **Come from** | Finishing learning tasks. Nothing else. | Appearing in the world after you finish a task, plus a few hidden ones per area |
| **Buy** | Lighting places, upgrades, bridges, lamps, gardens, drift isles | Outfits, decorations, creature and ship looks |
| **Never buy** | Portals to new realms, lessons | Anything that grows the world |
| **Kept by** | Bloom's core | The world's saved state |
| **Can be lost** | Never | Never. Uncollected crystals wait forever. |

No exchange between them. See file 2, B1.

## Proposed coin defaults (estimates to tune)

| Event | Coins | Shown as |
|---|---|---|
| Each light lit in a finished task | 2 | A five-light task pays 10 |
| Daily warm-up (two or three review items) | 2 each | "+6: three reviews, all fresh" |
| A spaced review item, anywhere | 2 | Full pay, as the Log requires |
| Finishing a lesson | 10 | |
| Improving on a past attempt | +3 | "+3: fewer retries than last time" |
| Getting a previously missed skill right | +3 | "+3: you got run-ons right after missing them" |
| Passing a delayed retention check | +5 | "+5: still solid after two weeks" |
| Completing a placement stop | 20, flat | Same whatever the score |
| Replaying the identical finished set | 5 once, then 0 | "Already earned" |

Coins are paid when the task finishes, not per first-try answer. A learner who needed three retries earns the same 10 as one who needed none. That is how "never pay less for doing worse" holds.

## How fast coins arrive (estimates)

| Learner | Tasks per week | Coins per week |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum pace: 3 sittings, about 5 tasks each | about 15 | about 170 |
| Daily: 7 sittings, about 6 tasks each | about 42 | about 450 |

The gap matters. A daily learner earns more than twice as fast. A curve tuned for one bores or starves the other. The design below handles this by making big moments depend on learning milestones, which coins cannot rush, and by having no ceiling.

## What things cost (estimates)

Your Log's target is "a new building takes about a week of regular use". At about 170 coins a week, that sets the first price.

| Thing | Price | At minimum pace |
|---|---|---|
| Small touches: a lamp, a planter, a banner | 20 to 60 | A sitting or two |
| A light bridge between isles | 80 | Half a week |
| **Lighting a place** (first level) | **180** | **About a week** |
| Place upgrade, level 2: a new activity type and richer scene | 300 | About two weeks |
| Place upgrade, level 3: the place's ongoing mode and its finest look | 500 | About three weeks |
| Castle upgrades, three of them | 400 each | |
| A drift isle (endless) | 400, rising gently to a cap of 600 | About three weeks each, forever |

**Every upgrade adds something to do,** never only a prettier model. Level 2 adds an activity type. Level 3 adds the ongoing mode. This keeps the Guide's rule that every place does something useful.

**A place being dim never blocks its lessons.** The sub-skills a dim place would practice are open on Bloom's Learn screen from the first day.

## Milestones: the big moments

These cannot be bought. Each is a short event with Amaris.

| Milestone | Opens |
|---|---|

| Finishing your first module | A second isle becomes available to light |

Under your new rule, these are the "big portals and new destinations". Everything in the price table above is a "small expansion".

## The growth curve: why it outlasts the content

**The risk you named:** the world runs out before the learning does. The opposite risk is just as real: coins keep arriving after everything is bought, and become meaningless.

Three layers, from finite to endless:

**Layer 1: hand-designed places.** For the writing realm:

| | Coins |
|---|---|
| 7 places × 3 levels (180 + 300 + 500) | 6,860 |
| 3 castle upgrades | 1,200 |
| About 40 small touches and bridges, averaging 60 | 2,400 |
| **Total** | **about 10,500** |

| Learner | Weeks to buy all of layer 1 |
|---|---|
| Minimum pace (170 a week) | about 62 |
| Daily (450 a week) | about 23 |

How long the writing curriculum itself takes is unknown until the template module exists. With roughly 38 sub-skills in the code today and the review ladder running out to 75 days, my rough guess is 8 to 12 months at minimum pace and 4 to 6 months daily. So layer 1 alone roughly keeps up at minimum pace and falls a little short for a daily learner. **These are estimates built on estimates.** They are here to show the method, not to be trusted as numbers.

**Layer 2: more realms.** Each new subject brings a realm of about the same size. Five subjects means about five times the content.

**Layer 3: drift isles, which never end.** Small isles float in from the distance. Each is generated from rules: its shape, plants, colors and layout come from a recipe plus a number unique to you, so no two are alike and no artist is needed. Each one, when lit:

- Adds a new spot for proofreading patrol, so it adds practice.
- Gives you a plot to decorate.
- Joins your skyline for good.

The price rises gently from 400 and stops at 600. It stops so that it never drifts out of reach: at minimum pace a new isle arrives about every three weeks, forever.

**Layer 4: the sky, which costs nothing.** Every day you practice adds one star to your sky. Every sub-skill you graduate adds a line between stars. Over a year this becomes a constellation that is yours alone. It is the Guide's "totals that grow" display, and it cannot run out because it is made of your own history.

**Reviews keep paying,** as you asked. Because graduated skills keep coming back on the long end of the ladder, a learner who has finished every lesson still earns coins every week, and layer 3 is always there to spend them on.

## Come-back-later moments (measured experiment, as in the Log)

- Lighting a place takes effect at once. An **upgrade** finishes "by tomorrow": the scaffolding of light is up when you leave and gone when you return.
- Lagoon flowers open overnight.
- Nothing is ever lost by being late. A flower that opened stays open for a month or a year.
- There is no speed-up for coins or crystals. Selling a way past waiting would be the first step toward the things on your Never build list.

## Surprise rewards (measured experiment, as in the Log)

Rare, free, never for sale and never on a schedule: a far-star visitor leaves a gift, an unusual creature passes through and can be invited to stay. They are triggered by finishing tasks, at random and rarely.

## What keeps the economy honest

- **No daily login reward.** It would pay for opening the app, not for learning.
- **No selling items back.** It invites loops that produce coins without learning.
- **No interest, no gambling, no paid random boxes.**
- **No coins from roaming, talking or decorating.**
- **One ledger.** Every coin has a dated reason you can read. If a number ever looks wrong, the ledger shows why.
- **Tuning rule:** watch how many weeks pass between "things I could afford". If you can always afford everything, prices are too low. If more than about two weeks pass at your real pace with nothing affordable, they are too high.

## Known tension to record in the Log

Your conflict rule makes you choose one whole copy when the phone and the server both have new work. Whichever you do not choose, its coins are gone. With one phone and the icon only, this should be rare. I recommend keeping the rule and writing this down, so it is a known trade and not a surprise.
