Hardest to undo first. Each has my recommendation and a short reply you can send back. **Do the phone test in file 7 before answering 1 to 4.** It is the evidence for them.

## The big ones

### 1. Which route

- **My recommendation: the hybrid.** Painted portraits, painted halls and painted backdrops where your eye rests. 3D and code under your feet. A few solid 3D objects made from pictures.
- **Why:** it gets closest to your photos, uses only tools whose free plans I could confirm, needs no rig and no computer, and stays well inside the phone budget.
- **Why it is hard to undo:** art made for one route mostly cannot be reused in another.
- **Would change my mind:** the phone test showing free picture tools cannot hold your bar, or a free 3D tool doing far better than I expect.
- **Reply:** "Route: hybrid", "Route: painted only" or "Route: 3D".

### 2. Beauty where you stand, or freedom everywhere

This is the most important call in the package, and it is where I push back hardest.

- **You asked for** art as close as possible to your photos **and** a full 360-degree camera with open roaming. With free tools on a phone these pull in opposite directions. A painting has no back.
- **My recommendation:** **a gently swinging view in halls and at viewpoints, where the art is richest. A free 360-degree view only in open-air areas, at the third roaming stage, with simpler art.**
- **Why it is hard to undo:** every painting made for a leashed view is useless for a free one.
- **Reply:** "Camera: leash in halls, free outdoors later", or "Camera: free everywhere, I accept simpler art".

### 3. One rendering style for every character

- Your references mix three: photo-real, soft illustration and comic line art.
- **My recommendation: painterly realism.** Real proportions and faces, soft brushwork. It avoids faces that drift toward real people, forgives small flaws and sits well in a glowing world.
- **Why it is hard to undo:** changing style later means repainting the whole cast.
- **Reply:** "Style: painterly realism", "Style: photo-real" or "Style: illustrated".

### 4. How characters stand in the world

- **My recommendation: painted figures that glide,** with front and back views and gentle code-made motion. Faces are shown large in the speech panel, where detail matters.
- **The alternative,** a 3D body made from the painting, is worth one try in the phone test and nothing more for now.
- **Reply:** "Figures: painted" or "Figures: test 3D first".

### 5. The center of the skin-tone range

- The first style guide recorded a center of medium golden-brown, from your written brief. Most of your new references are a deeper brown.
- This is yours to set. You said you want to see yourself in these characters, and I should not guess.
- **My recommendation:** tell me which reference is closest to the center, and I will record a new value from your own test pictures once you have some you love.
- **Reply:** "Skin center: as first recorded" or "Skin center: deeper, like the [name the photo] reference".

### 6. Will Bloom ever be shared or sold?

- Several free plans allow personal use only, or keep ownership of what you make. Using them now would mean redoing art later.
- **My recommendation: act as if Bloom may be shared one day.** Use only tools whose free terms do not forbid it, keep an art log and never use a tool that keeps ownership.
- **Why it is hard to undo:** a license cannot be fixed after the fact.
- **Reply:** "Licensing: keep sharing possible" or "Licensing: personal only is fine".

## The medium ones

### 7. An art log and a credits screen

- One line per art and sound file: tool, date, the words used, license, credit owed. A plain Credits screen in Bloom later.

- **Reply:** "Art log: yes".

### 8. Outfits and the crystal shop

- You approved crystals for cosmetic items such as outfits. With painted characters **every outfit is a whole new set of paintings.** Free tools cannot do mix-and-match clothing on a detailed character.
- **Recommendation:** crystals buy things that are cheap to make and lovely to own: glows and trails, decorations, companions and a small number of complete "looks" for your avatar. Not single garments.
- **Reply:** "Cosmetics: looks and effects, not garments" or tell me what you would rather.

## Things you did not ask about that I think are missing

- **A cap on scope.** Painted art invites endless polishing. Agree now that the first slice is one place and two characters.
- **Your time.** You become the art director. Each character is about six pictures and each place about three, with several tries each. Budget an hour or two per place. If that sounds like a chore, say so now.
- **A style drift check.** Every tenth picture, put it next to the first one. Art tools change, and so does taste.
- **An honest stop rule.** If free tools cannot reach your bar in the phone test, the real choices are a small paid tool or a human artist. That breaks your free-only rule, so it would be your decision alone.
