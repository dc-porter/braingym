# 8. What I verified and what I could not

Nov 16, 2026 · for Dee

Free plans and licenses change quickly. This is the full list of what stands behind the claims in this package, so you know which ones to trust and which to re-check.

**How I checked.** Small helper tasks read each tool's own web page and reported only what the page said, with short quotes. I then searched for what was blocked. I made every judgment myself.

**Three levels**

- **Checked:** read on the tool's or source's own page on Nov 16, 2026.
- **Reported:** the own page was blocked, empty or silent. The fact comes from search results or third-party write-ups, which often disagree. A lead, not a fact.
- **Not checked:** I could not confirm it either way.

## D. One legal point you should know

This is not legal advice.

A tool's terms decide whether **you may use** a picture. They do not decide whether **you own** it in a way the law will defend. As reported in section B, the U.S. position is that a picture made by AI from a text request alone has no human author and so no copyright. In practice: you can use your AI-made art, but you probably cannot stop someone else from copying it. For a personal app this does not matter. If Bloom were ever sold, it might.

## E. Things I know from experience but did not test today

Stated plainly so they are not mistaken for checked facts.

- How well each picture tool keeps a character the same across pictures.
- The typical flaws of picture-to-3D models: faces, hands, hair, thin parts. These are well known and visible in the tools' own galleries, but I did not make a model today.
- That the open depth and background tools will run in a cloud run. The basic image tools are present in my workspace and I used them today. The depth tool I did not try.
- That code-made motion makes a painted figure feel alive. It has not been tried on your art.

## F. Things only your phone can verify

- Whether the art reaches your bar. That is the test in file 7.
- Whether the public 3D sites work in Safari on your phone.
- Frame rate with painted layers.
- Sound, and the ring/silent switch.

## G. Measured here today

- Add-on sizes for the 3D library, version 0.187.0: model loader and helper 35 KB, shape unpacker 8 KB, texture unpacker 275 KB, glow effect 6 KB, all compressed.
- File sizes of paintings saved as WebP, using four of your references in my scratch area only: 50 to 410 KB at about 1,024 pixels wide, 110 to 930 KB at about 2,048.
- Graphics memory by arithmetic: about 8 MB for a 1,024 by 1,500 picture and 30 to 37 MB for 2,048 by 3,000.
