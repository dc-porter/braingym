# 3. Honest quality forecast

Nov 16, 2026 · for Dee

How close can each route get to your reference photos, and where does each fall short? These are my judgments as estimates, not measurements. The phone test in file 7 is how you check them with your own eyes.

## First, the hard truth about the photos

Your references are single still paintings. Each one was made to be looked at from exactly one spot, with unlimited time to render every strand of hair and every crystal. A game has to redraw its picture 60 times a second on a phone, from wherever the camera happens to be.

So there are two different targets hiding in "look like my photos":

1. **Look like them when I stand still and look.** This is reachable.
2. **Look like them from every angle while I roam freely.** This is not reachable with free tools on a phone, and is hard even for large studios with budgets.

Every route below is a different trade between those two.

## The scale I use

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Very close** | Side by side with your photo, a friend would say "same world" |
| **Close** | Clearly the same style, visibly simpler |
| **Partway** | The right colors and shapes, but it reads as a game, not a painting |
| **Far** | The idea is there, the beauty is not |

## Where each route falls short

### Route 1: painted 2D

- **The camera is on a leash.** Painted layers work when the view swings a little, about 20 to 30 degrees each way (estimated). Beyond that you see that the buildings are flat.
- **Characters do not turn smoothly.** A standee shows its front or its back. Turning is a flip, not a rotation.
- **No walking legs.** Characters glide. Your approved style already says this, but painted figures make it permanent.
- **Every new pose is a new painting,** and the art tool may change the face a little each time.
- **Every outfit is a new painting.** See decision 8 in file 9.

### Route 2: 3D from pictures

- **Faces are the worst part,** and faces are what you care about most. Up close they look soft and waxy, with painted-on eyes.
- **Long hair becomes a solid lump.** The flowing platinum hair in your references is the single hardest thing for these tools.
- **Jewelry and fine trim vanish.**
- **Crystal comes out opaque.** The tool cannot make see-through glass. The sparkle has to be rebuilt in code.
- **The free tools are few and limited,** as file 2 shows. You would get a handful of tries a day.
- **To walk, a character needs a rig,** and I found no free rigging that works from an iPhone.
- **A realistic model that moves stiffly looks worse than a stylized one.** The more lifelike the face, the more a frozen expression disturbs.

### Hybrid

- **It is two worlds stitched together,** and the seam can show: a richly painted character standing on a simpler 3D floor. Shared colors, shared light and soft haze hide most of it. The phone test will tell you whether it bothers you.
- **The camera stays on a leash inside halls and wherever the backdrop matters.**
- **Full 360-degree roaming gets the simplest art.**

## What no free route can do

Better to know now.

- Lifelike 3D people who walk, talk and change expression in real time.
- Hair and cloth that move like real hair and cloth.
- A whole city you can walk through with the detail of your white-city photo.
- A wardrobe of mix-and-match outfits on a detailed character.
- A different face angle for every moment of a conversation.

## What will look better than you expect

- **Portraits.** A painted portrait in the speech panel can look exactly as good as your character photos, because it is the same kind of picture.
- **Halls.** A painted interior with a depth map and a few code-made light rays is the cheapest, most beautiful thing in this whole plan.
- **The sky.** Painted moon and clouds over a code-made glow.
- **Small figures.** A character standing in the world on a phone is about as tall as your thumbnail. Her face is the size of a grain of rice. At that size a good painting and a perfect model look the same, which is why the money shot is the portrait, not the figure.

## How sure am I

| Claim | Confidence | What would change my mind |
|---|---|---|
| Painted portraits and halls can reach "very close" | High | The free picture tools giving you muddy or inconsistent results in the phone test |
| Free picture-to-3D faces will disappoint you | High | A free tool I could not test turning out much better than the published examples |
| The stitched seam in the hybrid is acceptable | Medium | Your own eyes on the first in-scene test |
| Characters stay consistent across several paintings | Medium | Faces drifting between the front, back and portrait pictures |
| An iPhone 14 holds 60 frames a second with painted layers | High | Stacking more than about four full-screen see-through layers |

## The honest summary

You can have a world that looks very close to your photos **when you stop and look,** in conversations, in halls and at viewpoints. You can have a world you **roam freely** that is clearly in the same style but simpler. With free tools you cannot have both in the same place at the same time. The hybrid gives you each one where it matters most.
